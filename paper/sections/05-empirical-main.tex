\section{Empirical Calibration and Systemic Contagion in the 2023 Banking Crisis}
\label{sec:empirical-calibration}

Sections~\ref{sec:weyl-heisenberg}--\ref{sec:duality-hedging} establish the
theoretical framework. The empirical bridge is Quantum State Tomography (QST):
it maps observable macro-financial time series onto the latent density operator
$\rho_t$, which feeds both the coherence early-warning diagnostic and the
option-pricing calibration. We organize the empirical analysis around three
questions that directly correspond to the three structural anomalies identified
in the Introduction: (i)~Can the quantum coherence signal anticipate the
systemic contagion of the 2023 banking crisis? (ii)~Does the semiclassical
skew expansion fit the observed implied-volatility surface? (iii)~Does the
quantum optimal hedge reduce hedging errors in the relevant regimes?

\subsection{Data and Reconstruction Methodology}\label{sec:data-methodology}

Our empirical panel spans a set of macro-financial time series covering the U.S.\
banking sector and broad market conditions. Three tiers of observables are used:

\paragraph{Tier 1 (Equity and ETF prices).}
Daily closing prices for JPMorgan Chase (JPM), Bank of America (BAC), Citigroup (C),
Goldman Sachs (GS), Morgan Stanley (MS), and Wells Fargo (WFC), together with three
financial-sector ETFs: the SPDR S\&P Regional Banking ETF (KRE), the SPDR S\&P Bank
ETF (KBE), and the Financial Select Sector SPDR ETF (XLF). This panel of six major
US banks and three financial-sector ETFs covers the constituent breadth of the U.S.\
banking system relevant for the 2023 regional banking crisis. Data sourced from
Yahoo Finance.

\paragraph{Tier 2 (Macro-financial stress indicators).}
Daily series from the Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED) system: the VIX index
(VIXCLS), the St.~Louis Fed Financial Stress Index (STLFSI4, weekly), the ICE BofA
US High Yield Master II Option-Adjusted Spread (BAMLH0A0HYM2), the Effective Federal
Funds Rate (EFFR), and the TED spread. For dates after FRED's TED discontinuation
(2022-01-22), we splice the TED series using SOFR $-$ EFFR as a documented L2 proxy.

\paragraph{Tier 3 (Microstructure observables).}
Amihud illiquidity, constructed from daily returns and dollar volume across the
Tier 1 equity panel, provides a market-microstructure proxy for Kyle's price impact
parameter $\lambda$, from which we calibrate $\hbar_{\mathrm{econ}}$ per the
theoretical relation $\hbar_{\mathrm{econ}} = \lambda \cdot q_0$.

\begin{definition}[Market state tomography / Quantum State Tomography (QST)]\label{def:mst}
Let $\{O_k\}_{k=1}^K$ be a set of market observables with empirical expectations
$\hat{\omega}_k = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T O_k(t)$. The \emph{market state
tomography} (MST), equivalently \emph{Quantum State Tomography} (QST) in the
terminology of Section~\ref{sec:introduction}, problem is to find the density operator
$\rho \in \mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_\omega)$ that minimizes the reconstruction error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mst}
    \hat{\rho} = \arg\min_{\rho \succeq 0, \, \operatorname{Tr}(\rho) = 1}
    \sum_{k=1}^K \left| \operatorname{Tr}(\rho \hat{O}_k) - \hat{\omega}_k \right|^2
    + \eta \operatorname{Tr}(\rho \ln \rho),
\end{equation}
where $\hat{O}_k$ are the GNS representations of the observables
(Definition~\ref{def:gns}), and $\eta > 0$ is a regularization parameter that
controls the entropy of the reconstructed state. The second term is the von Neumann
entropy regularizer, ensuring the reconstruction is robust to finite-sample noise
and prevents overfitting.
\end{definition}

The MST problem \eqref{eq:mst} is a convex optimization over the space of density
operators. For the single-qubit parametrization employed in this paper, the density
operator takes the form
\begin{equation*}
    \rho = \frac{1}{2} \left( \mathbf{1} + \mathbf{r} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma} \right),
\end{equation*}
where $\mathbf{r} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the Bloch vector with $\|\mathbf{r}\| \leq 1$,
and $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = (\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z)$ are the Pauli matrices.
The optimization reduces to a three-dimensional convex problem solvable via
projected gradient descent on the Bloch ball. To ensure the Bloch norm remains
strictly bounded away from unity (avoiding degenerate pure-state artifacts), we
apply a smooth $\tanh$ normalization: $\mathbf{r} = \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{\mathrm{raw}}
\cdot \tanh(\|\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{raw}}\|/s)$ where the scale parameter $s = 2.0$
is calibrated to preserve dynamic range while eliminating clipping artifacts.

The Pauli-observable mapping assigns each macro-financial series to one of three
non-commuting stress axes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\sigma_x$ (market stress axis): mean of standardized (VIX, STLFSI4, TED)
    \item $\sigma_y$ (credit stress axis): standardized HY-OAS
    \item $\sigma_z$ (rates/equity axis): $0.5 \cdot (\text{EFFR}_z
        - \text{GSPC returns}_z)$, so that positive $r_z$ corresponds to monetary
        tightening plus equity sell-off
\end{itemize}
HY-OAS from the FRED BAMLH0A0HYM2 series is available in our sample only from
2023-05-22 onwards. For dates prior to this, $r_y$ is set to zero (documented
absence of credit signal in the sub-sample). \emph{This has a direct consequence
for the coherence measure}: during the SVB and Signature Bank crisis window
(March 2023), the coherence $\mathcal{C}(\rho_t) = \sqrt{r_x^2 + r_y^2}$
reduces to $|r_x|$---a single-axis equity--volatility signal rather than a
full two-axis non-commutative measure. The reported coherence results for the
SVB and Signature events therefore reflect a conservative, lower-bound estimate
of the true non-commutative coherence that would obtain with complete credit
data; the First Republic Bank event (May 1, 2023) does have HY-OAS coverage and
represents the cleanest test of the full framework.

The calibration proceeds in two stages. First, the economic Planck constant
$\hbar_{\mathrm{econ}}$ is calibrated from the Tier 3 microstructure observables
using $\hbar_{\mathrm{econ}} = \lambda \cdot q_0$, where $\lambda$ is estimated
via Amihud illiquidity; the resulting series is linearly rescaled so that its
full-sample mean equals 0.062, a normalisation anchor consistent with the
microstructural range $[0.01, 0.15]$ discussed in Remark~\ref{rem:units}
(the 0.062 level is not independently estimated but chosen to anchor the scale).
Second, the Bloch vector $\mathbf{r}_t$ is estimated
daily by solving \eqref{eq:mst} conditional on the calibrated
$\hbar_{\mathrm{econ}}$.

\begin{remark}[Statistical power caveat]\label{rem:stat-power}
The crisis sample comprises three failure events (SVB, Signature Bank, FRB).
With $N_{\mathrm{crisis}} = 3$, a one-sided binomial test cannot achieve the
$5\%$ significance level ($p_{\min} = 0.125$); the empirical findings must
therefore be interpreted as \emph{proof-of-concept} evidence rather than
formal statistical inference. Time-series results (Granger causality, unit-root
tests) rest on the full sample from October 2004 to December 2024 ($T \approx 5{,}096$ trading days)
and retain standard asymptotic validity.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Capturing Systemic Contagion: The 2023 Banking Crisis}
\label{sec:coherence-2023}

With $\rho_t$ reconstructed daily (Section~\ref{sec:data-methodology}), we
turn to the first empirical question: can the quantum coherence signal anticipate
the 2023 banking crisis before conventional indicators register stress?
The empirical content of our framework is carried by the off-diagonal elements of
the reconstructed density matrix. Off-diagonal entries of $\rho$ encode systemic
contagion through the algebraic structure of the observable algebra $\mathcal{A}$,
while diagonal entries track marginal distress probabilities. In the single-qubit
parametrization, $\rho_{12} = \rho_{21}^*$ measures \emph{quantum coherence} between
distressed and non-distressed sectors \citep{baumgratz2014quantifying}. We interpret
this coherence as the algebraic signature of the non-commutative order-flow channel:
institutions linked through $\hat{P}$ acquire entangled distress states in the GNS
representation, with off-diagonal components of $\rho$ serving as the empirical
handle.

\begin{definition}[Quantum coherence measure]\label{def:coherence}
The \emph{quantum coherence} of the market state $\rho$ is defined as the
$\ell_1$-norm of the off-diagonal elements \citep{baumgratz2014quantifying}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coherence}
    \mathcal{C}(\rho) = \sum_{i \neq j} |\rho_{ij}|.
\end{equation}
For the single-qubit parametrization, $\mathcal{C}(\rho) = \sqrt{r_x^2 + r_y^2}$,
where $r_x$ and $r_y$ are the transverse components of the Bloch vector. The
coherence $\mathcal{C}(\rho)$ quantifies the degree of quantum superposition (off-diagonal coherence) between
the distressed and non-distressed sectors of the financial system.
\end{definition}

against the 30-day rolling Pearson correlation of KRE (Regional Banking ETF)
log-returns and VIX log-changes from January to June~2023. The KRE--VIX pair is
chosen as the classical benchmark because it captures the cross-sector leverage
effect (equity vs.~volatility index) that intuitively should track crisis
contagion; the KRE--S\&P~500 pair, in contrast, exhibits naturally high correlation
throughout the window and is thus uninformative as a crisis-onset signal.

threshold crossed May~1; see Remark~\ref{rem:stat-power} for the
proof-of-concept interpretation). This event, which has full
HY-OAS coverage and therefore represents the complete two-axis non-commutative
measure, constitutes the core empirical evidence for the advance-warning capacity
of the coherence channel.

This same-day-vs-multiday contrast has a physical interpretation. At the
onset of a crisis, the quantum coherence signal captures the cross-sector state
of the market (off-diagonal density matrix elements linking distressed and
non-distressed sectors), while the Pearson correlation of price signals captures
only the ex-post co-movement of realized returns. During the pre-crisis buildup
week (early March 2023), both signals are triggered by the same market stress,
but $\mathcal{C}(\rho_t)$'s ``dark contagion'' channel---the microstructural
order-flow signal encoded in $\hat{P}$---anticipates the ex-post correlation
regime shift by capturing the latent cross-sector coupling before it manifests
as realized price co-movement.

\subsection{The Von Neumann Entropy as a Forward-Looking Risk-Regime Barometer}
\label{sec:entropy-2023}

The coherence signal of Section~\ref{sec:coherence-2023} captures contagion through
the \emph{off-diagonal} structure of $\rho_t$. We now ask whether the \emph{scalar
summary} of the full density matrix---the von Neumann entropy $S(\rho_t) =
-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_t \log \rho_t)$---provides an even earlier warning by
measuring overall regime uncertainty rather than cross-sector linkage.
The von Neumann entropy collapses the multi-dimensional quantum state into a
single scalar that tracks systemic uncertainty.

\begin{definition}[Von Neumann entropy of the market state]\label{def:vn-entropy}
The von Neumann entropy of the market state $\rho$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vn-entropy}
    S(\rho) = -\operatorname{Tr}(\rho \ln \rho) = -\sum_i \lambda_i \ln \lambda_i,
\end{equation}
where $\{\lambda_i\}$ are the eigenvalues of $\rho$. For the single-qubit
parametrization with Bloch vector $\mathbf{r}$,
\begin{equation*}
    S(\rho) = -\frac{1+\|\mathbf{r}\|}{2} \ln \frac{1+\|\mathbf{r}\|}{2}
    - \frac{1-\|\mathbf{r}\|}{2} \ln \frac{1-\|\mathbf{r}\|}{2}.
\end{equation*}
The entropy ranges from $S(\rho) = 0$ (pure state, $\|\mathbf{r}\| = 1$, complete
certainty about the market regime) to $S(\rho) = \ln 2 \approx 0.693$ (maximally
mixed state, $\|\mathbf{r}\| = 0$, complete uncertainty).
\end{definition}

$S(\rho)$ admits a natural economic reading. A pure state ($S \approx 0$) places
the system firmly in one regime---distressed or not. A mixed state
($S \approx \ln 2$) signals superposition: the market cannot simultaneously
resolve price and order-flow uncertainty (Theorem~\ref{thm:uncertainty}), and this
structural ambiguity registers as elevated entropy.

\begin{remark}[Empirical Finding: Granger causality of von Neumann entropy]\label{prop:granger-entropy}
In a bivariate VAR(4) model estimated on weekly first-differences of KRE
option-implied volatility ($\Delta\mathrm{IV}_t$) and von Neumann entropy
($\Delta S_t$) over the full 2004--2024 sample ($T \approx 1{,}040$ weekly
observations):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] $\Delta S_t$ Granger-causes $\Delta \text{IV}_t$ at the $1\%$
        significance level ($F$-statistic reported in OS~Section~\ref{OS-sec:hedging-performance}).
    \item[(b)] $\Delta \text{IV}_t$ does not Granger-cause $\Delta S_t$ at the
        $5\%$ level, confirming the one-directional predictive flow.
    \item[(c)] The cumulative impulse response of $\Delta \text{IV}_t$ to a
        one-standard-deviation shock in $\Delta S_t$ reaches its maximum at a
        lag of 3~weeks.
\end{enumerate}
These results indicate that the von Neumann entropy $S(\rho)$ contains
forward-looking information about implied volatility not already reflected in
current option prices. This is an empirical finding on a specific sample; its
generalizability to other crisis episodes is addressed in
Section~\ref{sec:robustness}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Economic interpretation of the entropy]\label{rem:entropy-econ}
The predictive power of $S(\rho)$ has a natural economic interpretation. The von
Neumann entropy measures the structural uncertainty that arises from the
non-commutative algebra of market observables. When this uncertainty is high, the
market is in a superposition of distressed and non-distressed regimes, and the
resolution of this superposition---through the realization of a systemic
event---generates a large movement in option implied volatilities. The entropy
therefore captures the latent fragility of the financial system before it
manifests in observed prices, providing a theoretically grounded early warning
indicator that is distinct from conventional volatility or correlation measures.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Robustness and Additional Results}\label{sec:robustness}

The coherence and entropy results of Sections~\ref{sec:coherence-2023}
and~\ref{sec:entropy-2023} address the first empirical question. The hedging
and calibration results below address the second and third. We also assess
whether all findings are stable across model specifications and data choices.
Full robustness tables, model comparisons, and hedging-performance details are in
Online Supplement~S.2. The key findings are as follows.

\paragraph{Stability of QST calibration.}
Three tests confirm robustness: (i)~perturbing each Pauli coefficient by $\pm 10\%$
yields a coefficient of variation for $\theta_t$ below $8\%$; (ii)~the median
$\theta_t$ is stable across non-overlapping 2004--2024 subsamples
(OS~Section~\ref{OS-sec:robustness-checks});
(iii)~a qutrit model correlates $0.93$ with the qubit estimates.

\paragraph{Cross-crisis comparison.}
The quantum distress amplitude $\theta_t$ peaks at $0.9993$ (GFC, 2008-10-15),
$0.9955$ (COVID, 2020-03-16), and $0.8687$ (2023 banking crisis, 2023-05-24),
providing clear quantitative discrimination across all three crises
(OS~Figure~\ref{OS-fig:indicator-comparison}).

\paragraph{Option surface calibration and hedging.}
Calibration to the KRE implied-volatility smile on March~10, 2023
($\hat\sigma_{60d} = 28.41\%$ from realized returns; the historical option chain
is not publicly available, so the smile shape is parameterized as
$\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}(K,\tau)=\sigma_{\mathrm{atm}}(\tau)+am+bm^2$ with
canonical banking-crisis parameters $a=-0.50$, $b=+1.00$; see
OS~Section~\ref{OS-sec:iv-surface} for details)
yields $\hbar_{\mathrm{econ}} = 0.087$ and $\gamma = 0.51$ (OS~Figure~\ref{OS-fig:iv-smile}).
The quantum optimal hedge achieves $555.9$~bps RMSHE versus BSM $560.1$~bps
and Heston $572.3$~bps (OS~Table~\ref{OS-tab:hedging-rmshe})
in the SVB stress window, with
the improvement concentrated in out-of-the-money regimes where
$\hbar_{\mathrm{econ}}\Gamma_{\mathrm{BS}}\gamma x/\tau$ is non-negligible.
The corresponding bootstrap confidence intervals are
reported in OS~Table~\ref{OS-tab:hedging-rmshe}; the absolute improvement is small but consistent
with the theoretical prediction that quantum corrections dominate only in
the OTM--short-maturity regime. A Diebold--Mariano test on the full-sample
daily hedge-error differential is deferred to future work; with $N=19$ days
in the SVB window, the current sample is insufficient for formal inference.

\paragraph{Time-series properties of $\hbar_{\mathrm{econ}}$ and $\theta_t$.}
Unit-root tests on the calibrated distress amplitude $\theta_t$ yield mixed
evidence: the ADF test rejects the unit-root null at the 1\% level
($p < 0.001$), while the KPSS test marginally rejects the stationarity null
($p = 0.035$), consistent with a near-integrated or fractionally integrated
process with strong persistence (AR(1) $\approx 0.76$ (0.7579)). The VIX-based proxy for
$\hbar_{\mathrm{econ}}$ exhibits a CUSUM statistic ($741.96$) far exceeding
the 5\% critical value ($100.58$), indicating structural change over the
2004--2024 sample. The rolling 252-day mean ranges from $0.110$ (January 2018)
to $0.150$ (July 2008), consistent with the time-varying nature of market
microstructure liquidity. These findings motivate interpreting $\hbar_{\mathrm{econ}}$
as a \emph{regime-conditional} friction parameter rather than a universal constant;
structural-break analysis is reported in Online Supplement~S.2.

The structural change in $\hbar_{\mathrm{econ}}(t)$ warrants a brief theoretical
comment. Kyle's $\lambda$ is itself an equilibrium object that depends on the
information environment; regime changes in market microstructure---such as
post-crisis liquidity fragmentation or the advent of zero-commission trading---can
shift $\lambda$ and hence $\hbar_{\mathrm{econ}}$. The CCR is therefore best
interpreted as a \emph{local} approximation valid within a microstructure regime,
with $\hbar_{\mathrm{econ}}(t)$ serving as a slowly-varying parameter estimated
over rolling windows. The detected break (2008 peak, 2018 trough) coincides with
known microstructure regime changes---the GFC liquidity crisis and the post-Volcker
rule era of reduced dealer balance sheets---supporting regime change as the
economic explanation rather than model misspecification.

\paragraph{In-sample caveat and future out-of-sample validation.}
All crisis events reported in this paper fall within the 2004--2024 calibration
window; the coherence and entropy thresholds are estimated on the full sample,
which introduces a look-ahead component. A rigorous out-of-sample evaluation
using an expanding-window protocol (calibration cut-off 2015, evaluation
2015--2024) is left for future work. The present results should be interpreted
as \emph{in-sample retrospective diagnostics} that demonstrate the framework's
capacity to retrospectively identify structural stress---not as a real-time
forecasting system. The theoretical advance-warning properties of the coherence
channel (particularly the multi-week FRB lead documented in
Section~\ref{sec:coherence-2023}) are interpreted as
providing \emph{proof-of-concept} evidence that the non-commutative operator
structure encodes crisis-related information before it manifests in price-level
co-movements, pending confirmation through out-of-sample replication.
